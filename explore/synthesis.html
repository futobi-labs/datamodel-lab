Data synthesis

Two thousand four hundred glass panes, stacked in the order data travels. Each pane is a cross-section of the platform at one depth. Fly through them and watch specks of raw data drift like sand, settle into tables, crystallize into facts and dimensions, join into a semantic model and finally surface as the visuals on a report.

    Reading the stack

    Depth is the journey. The first panes are the operational systems and the last are the reports, with every medallion layer taking its own band of panes in between. A pane shows where every grain of data is at that depth, so looking straight down the stack shows one layer as a cross-section, and pulling back shows the whole column of glass with the streams running through it.

    What the grains are

    Each grain is a thread through the stack that follows the real column lineage in the sample model. It enters as a loose speck, lands in a source column, then moves pane by pane to the next column it feeds, holding its place while it sits in a layer and sweeping sideways when a pipeline carries it to the next one. Grains that share a column pack together, so tables appear where grains settle and tighten as the data gets cleaner. Columns that are made rather than copied, such as surrogate keys and the date dimension, are born mid-stack. A grain whose column is dropped simply stops.

    The bright moving points are loads in motion. Send a load to push one batch through every source at once, follow a grain to ride along with one value and read its journal, or click any table to see only the grains that pass through it.

    Two honest caveats

    Grains are illustrative, not row counts: the sample model has structure but no data. And the layouts on each band are drawn for clarity, not taken from any tool. To watch what happens once a query reaches the model, open the semantic engine.
