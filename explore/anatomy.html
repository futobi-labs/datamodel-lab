    Reading the specimen

    The metaphor is not decoration. Each tissue does the same job its layer does, and the order is the order data travels: from the surface that touches the outside world, inward to the organs that do the work.

    What moves, and what changes it

    Vessels are the column lineage in the sample model, one vessel per column-to-column edge. A cell entering a vessel follows that edge, then picks one of the edges leaving the column it lands on, so flows thicken where many columns converge, like fact_sales.net_amount, and thin out where columns are dropped. Pass-through and rename edges leave a cell as it is. Real transforms, such as a cast, a surrogate key lookup, an SCD2 row hash or a DAX measure, flash at the joint and change the cell's shape: jagged raw specks in the skin, droplets in fat, smooth discs in muscle, crystals in bone, glowing orbs in the organs and sparks in the nerves.

    Pump a load to send one batch through every source at once, the way a scheduled pipeline does. Follow a cell to ride along with a single value and read its journal in the panel. Click any structure to dissect it: everything outside its lineage goes dark, and cells only travel the paths that feed it.

    Two honest caveats

    The cells are illustrative, not row counts: the sample model has structure but no data. And in a real platform the outer layers move on a schedule while the inner ones answer questions in milliseconds. The semantic engine exploration slows that second clock down so you can watch it.
