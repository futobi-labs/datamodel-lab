Bus matrix

Kimball's planning grid, drawn from the model rather than a spreadsheet. Rows are business processes (facts), columns are the dimensions they share. A full column is a conformed dimension; a half mark is a fact meeting a dimension at a coarser grain.

Click a cell

Each cell shows how a fact reaches a dimension: the key, the role, whether it is active, and the grain it meets it at.

Marks

  • Filled circle: active many-to-one at full grain
  • Number: role-playing; how many relationships
  • Half circle: coarser grain (shrunken dimension)
  • Ring: inactive only

Why draw it

The bus matrix is the cheapest design review there is. Read down a column to see which processes a dimension must serve, which is the list of people who need to agree on its definition. Read across a row to see what a report on that process can be sliced by. The empty cells are the questions you can't answer yet: here, nobody can ask for inventory by salesperson, and budget can't be cut by warehouse.
